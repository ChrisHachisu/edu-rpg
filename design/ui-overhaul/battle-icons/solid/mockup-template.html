Battle command icons: three routes to solid

The shipped glyphs were calibrated to the bottom tab bar, so they are hairline outlines at 22 px. That is why they read flimsy. Every candidate below is heavier than the tab family on purpose, which means adopting any of them breaks the match with the tab bar. Panel 3 shows exactly what that costs, on every candidate. Whether the tabs should follow, or whether the battle bar should deliberately read heavier because it is the primary combat control, is your call.

View at 100% browser zoom. Every icon on this page is at true device size, 22 px, except the one row that is explicitly labelled as magnified.

1 · Gilded Rail, Attack selected

The shipped HUD has no coloured button blocks, so each command's colour lives entirely in the glyph tint. Attack sits on the gold plate and inverts to the plate's ink.

2 · Same rail, Defend selected

Attack is now red and Defend has inverted to the plate ink. The tint moves with the selection, which is only possible because the sheet is still an alpha mask. A candidate with baked colour would fail here rather than look wrong.

3 · What it costs beside the tab bar

The shipped tab strip on top, the selected candidate directly under it, same 22 px and same gold. The two rows are meant to look like one family today. Flip the switcher and watch the bottom row pull away from the top one.

The Equip tab is a sword and the Items tab is a pouch. Both can be on screen in the same session as the battle bar, so Attack has to stay clearly not-that-sword. It keeps the diagonal angle, the broad leaf blade and the swept crescent guard on all three candidates; only the pommel changes, and only on candidate A, where a pure silhouette cannot hold an open ring.

4 · All four at once, no switching

This is the panel that decides it. The bottom row of each card is 22 px, the size a thumb actually sees. The magnified row above it is only there to judge the drawing, and art that reads at 96 px and mushes at 22 px has passed a review it should have failed.

5 · Measured

Stroke to optical size, measured on each candidate's own source pixels by the shipped measurement in scripts/build_battle_icons.py. It is the number the shipped set was ranked on. For the two filled candidates it stops meaning pen weight and starts meaning body mass, so ink share of the icon's own box is given beside it as the honest comparison.

Tab anchor band, measured on the tab family's own pixels: 5.91% to 6.67%. The shipped battle set was calibrated into it and landed at 5.92% to 6.24%, except Attack at 5.56%, which is the one glyph that is thinner than the rest and part of why the set reads uneven.
