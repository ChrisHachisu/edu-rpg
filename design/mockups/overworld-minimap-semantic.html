Overworld minimap, drawn from the semantic map

Not a recoloured tile grid. The terrain is one pre-baked smooth image derived from the map the game actually collides with; the minimap blits an 80-cell window out of it and draws landmarks as pins on top. Everything below renders at the true 146 px canvas inside the real 156 px gold frame, on the real #15161c.

What changed since the last round. The three rejected palettes all recoloured the same per-tile fillRect lattice. This is a different object: an image with organic coastlines and soft landforms, produced by the project's own build_semantic_map / smooth_owner_semantic machinery, with the invariant those scripts exist to hold. The class at every cell centre is preserved exactly, so collision is unchanged and the smooth edge only moves between centres. Measured on this bake: 0 of 128,000 cell centres wrong.

1 · The coordinate mapping

The semantic map covers Act 1 at 148×182. The live mapData that drawFieldMap reads is 320×400. The bridge is stated in the generated snapshot itself, src/map-engine/generated/act1RuntimeSnapshot.ts:

ACT1_RUNTIME_SNAPSHOT_WORLD_SIZE[320, 400]
ACT1_RUNTIME_SNAPSHOT_BOUNDS[16, 218, 163, 399]
ACT1_RUNTIME_SNAPSHOT_SIZE[148, 182]   = 163-16+1, 399-218+1

The same numbers are ACTS[1] in scripts/build_semantic_map.py:49 and the Act 1 bounds in owner-terrain.json. So the mapping is a pure translation, with no scale and no flip:

act1cell = (worldX − 16, worldY − 218)
semanticPixel = (act1cell × 16) in the 2368×2912 render

Proof, mechanical. Every one of the 26,936 cells of the Act 1 region in the cached runtime grid was compared against the snapshot under that offset: 26,933 identical. The 3 exceptions are at world (153, 318–320), where the snapshot carries the blocker override the cached grid predates. Landmark positions, taken from src/data/maps.ts and located in the labelled reference render by pin colour:

All three land inside a third of a cell. (Two more checked the same way: Millbrook 0.27, Crystal Cave 0.87.)

One thing does not line up, and it is not the terrain. semantic-maps/landmark-roster.json, the file _stamp_landmarks uses to stamp landmark discs into the semantic PNGs, puts every Act 1 landmark somewhere the shipped game has plain grass. Greenhollow is 85.7 cells away from where the game puts it, Whispering Woods 80.1, Millbrook 65.4, Port Sapphire 56.8. The terrain in those PNGs is truthful, because it is derived from the runtime grid; the pins stamped into it are not. So this mockup takes landmarks from the grid's own landmark tiles instead, which is also what the labelled reference render agrees with. Nothing here depends on the roster.

2 · Outside Act 1

There is no gap to handle. Five act regions already tile the whole 320×400 world and each has a runtime semantic map on main:

Rather than stitch five images and special-case the seams, this bake goes back one step to the shared source both the act maps and the game come from, generateOverworldMap(320, 400), cached at semantic-maps/runtime-overworld-grid.json, and bakes one image for the whole world, 320×400 cells at 6 px per cell. The Act 1 blocker overrides from the snapshot are applied first, so the bake is the final shipped map, not the pre-override one. The minimap window is then uniform everywhere: no act lookup, no seam, no missing region, and the existing sx/sy clamp already guarantees the window never leaves the image.

Textured land, routes drawn

recommended

The landmass carries a faint interior grain so it reads as terrain rather than as a flat chip, and the road network (path, bridge, darkPath) is drawn as a dim warm thread. Routes are a guidance layer with zero mask contribution, exactly as OVERWORLD-MOVEMENT-BOUNDARIES.md requires. Baked asset 406 KB.

4 · Variant B, Relief

Flat land, no routes

the quieter one

Same geometry, same pins, but the land is flat and no roads are drawn. Nothing on the map competes with the coastline, the blocking masses and the pins. Baked asset 162 KB, less than half of A. The trade: A tells you where to go, B tells you where you are and what is in the way.

5 · Reading the map

Value encodes walkability. Every walkable class is lighter than every blocking class, and water is darkest of all, so "can I get there" is answered by brightness before colour is read at all. The four semantic classes map one-to-one onto the shipped blocking rules in SHIPPED-BLOCKING-RULES.md: water, tree and mountain/wallBarrier block, everything else is walkable ground.

Canopy. The game's woods are scattered single trees at roughly half density, so the truthful class layer alone renders a wood as static. A soft density haze sits under the class layer: walkable ground inside a wood darkens toward the canopy colour while every actually blocking trunk still draws on top of it. It moves no boundary and changes no cell centre. The ordinal read survives: open ground lighter than canopy, canopy lighter than a trunk.

6 · Landmarks and blockers stop hiding

In the shipping table, tile 9 (portal) is #33cc66 grass-green, tile 10 (haunted portal) is #226622 against forest #225522, and tile 14 (wall barrier, a hard blocker) is #1a4d1a, another green. The camera below stands west of the Act 2 wall barrier at (230, 248), looking at the barrier, both haunted portals beyond it, and the dense forest flanking it. Four different things in four near-identical greens.

Here every landmark is a pin drawn on top of the terrain at a fixed size, never a terrain colour, and each pin carries a dark seat so its legibility never depends on what is underneath. Fixed size is also what stops tiles 11, 19 and 20 from being two-pixel specks: the world's only special cave is a pin, not a tile.

The hero is the one mark that must never be mistaken for a landmark, so it is not a pin at all but a reticle, a bright core inside its own ring. Nothing else on the map is a ring around a dot, so finding yourself never depends on telling one pin shape from another. That matters more than it sounds: an earlier flat square, tried at a camera centred between the two haunted portals, vanished into them completely.

7 · Cost per draw

The shipping drawFieldMap issues 6,402 fillRect calls per draw, up to 4.5 times a second on the main thread. The baked version issues one drawImage plus one pin per visible landmark. Both renderers below run on the same 146 px canvas at the same devicePixelRatio, against the same real world data, in this browser.
